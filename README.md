Spot trading is the simplest starting point for many new users. You buy or sell an asset at the current market price or place a limit order. Spot trading does not create the same liquidation mechanics as leveraged derivatives, although the asset itself can still lose value.

A fee rebate is easiest to understand in spot markets because the fee is attached directly to the executed order.

### Margin trading

Margin trading allows users to borrow against collateral. That introduces borrowing costs, liquidation risk, and additional account rules. A trading-fee promotion does not remove interest charges or protect collateral.

New users searching for a bonus should usually understand spot trading first before considering margin.

### Futures and perpetual contracts

Futures and perpetual contracts can provide leverage and allow long or short positions. They also introduce funding fees, maintenance margin requirements, and liquidation risk.

The signup offer should not be treated as a reason to use leverage. A fee discount is a small cost adjustment compared with the possible effect of a leveraged price move.

Options have their own pricing, expiration, settlement, and risk characteristics. Fee schedules may differ from spot trading. Some account modes do not support every options function, and eligibility can depend on region and account settings.
